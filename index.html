A craft very few still practice

Ships in bottles were once a pastime of sailors on long voyages. Today very few people still build them by hand, because there is no shortcut: every hull, mast and sail has to be shaped small enough to pass through the neck of a bottle, then raised and set in place inside the glass.

Wil started with a love of woodworking and a simple thought. The bottles he came across along the coast were too beautiful to throw away. What if each one held a boat?

Every bottle is rescued and upcycled from here on the Emerald Coast. Its shape decides the boat: some stand upright, some lie on their side, and no two are ever the same.

From raw wood to glass

Nothing here comes from a kit. Each boat is shaped, painted and rigged by hand, then built inside the bottle through its neck.

  1. The bottle chooses the boat

    The shape and size of the glass decide what fits: a slim sloop for a tall bottle, a tall ship or liner for a wide one.

  2. Carving the hull

    The hull is cut and shaped from a block of raw wood, then sanded smooth by hand.

  3. Building the details

    Decks, cabins, cannons and portholes are added piece by piece, at a scale where every millimeter shows.

  4. Painting by hand

    Every stripe, porthole and waterline is painted with a fine brush.

  5. Masts, sails and rigging

    Masts and spars are whittled to size, sails are cut and painted, and the rigging is tied thread by thread.

  6. Into the glass

    The boat goes in through the neck and is set in place inside, then mounted on a wooden stand with a name plate burned into the wood.

Questions

How big are the pieces?

Each piece is as big as its bottle. Most use a standard wine or spirits bottle, roughly 10 to 14 inches long, mounted on a wooden stand.

How long does a custom boat take?

It depends on the detail. Wil will give you a delivery date with your quote, so let him know early if it's for a birthday or holiday.

Can I add a name or a message?

Yes. Every piece has a wooden name plate, and Wil burns your text into it by hand. A name, a date or a short phrase works best, since the plate is small.

Can I use my own bottle?

Often, yes. A bottle from a wedding, an anniversary or a special trip makes the piece even more personal. Send a photo and Wil will tell you if the neck is wide enough.

How do I pay?

You'll agree on the price before any work begins. A deposit starts the build and the rest is due on delivery.

How do I care for it?

Keep it out of direct sun to protect the paint and sails, and dust the glass with a soft dry cloth.
